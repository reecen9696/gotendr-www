Where do you find landscape tenders in Sydney and NSW?

Landscape tenders in NSW and Sydney are found on buy.nsw for state work, on council websites and portals such as VendorPanel, TenderLink and Tenders.net for council work, on EstimateOne for private commercial packages from builders, and on AusTender for Commonwealth work. Aggregators republish public tenders with paid alerts.

Which kinds of landscape tenders are there in NSW?

Landscape tenders in NSW come from two separate markets, and each one is found in a different place. The first is public work: tenders issued by NSW Government agencies and local councils. The second is private commercial work: landscape packages that head contractors send out to subcontractors on a building project.

Public tenders are often for landscape construction, streetscapes, parks and open space, and grounds maintenance contracts. They are advertised openly, carry formal returnable schedules, and are evaluated against published criteria.

Private commercial tenders are the landscape package on an apartment block, a school, an aged care home or a commercial fitout. The builder invites a shortlist of landscape subcontractors to price the package, then carries the winning number in their own head contract price.

Knowing which market you want to work in tells you where to look. A maintenance contractor chasing council grounds contracts and a construction subcontractor chasing builder packages will spend their time on almost entirely different sites.

Where are landscape tenders in NSW advertised?

NSW Government landscape tenders are published on buy.nsw. buy.nsw is the NSW Government's procurement platform, and it absorbed the old NSW eTendering system: tenders.nsw.gov.au now redirects to it. If you have an old bookmark or login for eTendering, start again from buy.nsw.

buy.nsw has listed grounds maintenance and turf management tenders, as well as broader open space and construction opportunities that include landscape scope. Register as a supplier, then search by keyword and category rather than browsing, because landscape work is often described as grounds, turf, open space, streetscape or public domain rather than landscaping.

State tenders are usually documented formally. Expect a request for tender with conditions, a specification, drawings where there is construction, and returnable schedules that must be completed in the format issued.

How do NSW councils advertise landscape work?

NSW councils advertise their tenders on their own websites and through third-party portals such as VendorPanel, TenderLink and Tenders.net. There is no single council portal in NSW, so each council's tenders page is the authoritative place to confirm where it publishes.

Council work is where much of the park, playground, streetscape and grounds maintenance tendering happens. Some councils also run panels, which are pre-approved supplier lists that later receive quotes or mini-tenders without a fresh public advertisement.

Metropolitan Sydney has many councils close together, so a Sydney subcontractor can realistically watch a cluster of them at once. Regional NSW councils cover larger areas with fewer, more spread-out projects, so travel, accommodation and local supply become part of whether a tender is worth chasing at all.

  1. List the councils you can service without travel eroding your margin.
  2. Find each council's tenders page and note which portal it uses.
  3. Register on each portal once, using the same business details.
  4. Save alerts on each portal for your categories.
  5. Check each council's page for panel invitations as well as open tenders.

Where do you find landscape tenders in Sydney from builders?

Most private landscape tenders in Sydney are found on EstimateOne. EstimateOne (E1) is an Australian construction tendering platform that connects head contractors with subcontractors and suppliers, and it has a landscaping tenders category. Builders load their projects, and subcontractors download the documents for the trade packages they want to price.

The rest of Sydney's private commercial work comes as direct invitations. Builders who have used you before, or who have seen your pricing on another job, send the landscape package straight to you. That list grows from submitting clear, complete, well-qualified tenders every time, win or lose.

A builder's package is different from a council tender. It is usually priced against the builder's own drawings, specification and trade breakdown, often with a short deadline, and the builder compares several landscape prices side by side. A clear read of the scope before pricing is what stops your number looking cheap for the wrong reasons.

Are tender aggregators worth using?

A tender aggregator is a paid service that collects public tenders from many government and council sources and republishes them by category and region, with email alerts. The main ones covering NSW are Australian Tenders, illion TenderLink and Tenders.net.

Aggregators save time when you chase public work across many councils, because one set of alerts replaces checking each council separately. They do not replace registering on the original portal: you still download documents, ask questions and lodge your response where the tender was issued.

Whether one is worth paying for comes down to how much public work you want and how many councils you cover. If you only bid for a few nearby councils and builder packages, direct alerts may be enough.

What about Commonwealth and Canberra work?

Australian Government tenders are published on AusTender, the Commonwealth's tendering site. Work for Australian Government agencies in NSW is advertised there rather than on buy.nsw, so add AusTender to your alerts if you want Commonwealth grounds or landscape contracts.

Canberra sits outside NSW procurement altogether, so ACT Government work is not advertised on buy.nsw. If you work in southern NSW and across the border, treat Canberra as its own market; our Canberra page covers how we price packages there.

How do you set up alerts for landscape tenders in NSW?

A tender alert is a saved search that emails you when a new tender matches your categories and region. Setting them up once on every channel you use is the difference between seeing a tender in time to price it properly and seeing it two days before it closes.

  1. Register on buy.nsw, EstimateOne, AusTender and each council portal you need.
  2. Save searches for landscaping, grounds maintenance, streetscape and open space, not just "landscape".
  3. Filter by region: metropolitan Sydney, the areas of regional NSW you service, or both.
  4. Send every alert to one shared inbox that someone checks daily.
  5. Review the searches every few months and remove the ones that only bring noise.

Keywords matter because buyers describe the same work in different ways. A streetscape upgrade, a public domain package and a park renewal can all be mostly landscape scope under a title that never uses the word.

How do you decide which tenders to bid?

Finding tenders is the easy part; choosing the right ones is where the margin is. Before pricing, run each tender through a quick bid or no-bid check of scope, competition, fit, risk and deadline, which is set out in full in is a landscape tender worth bidding.

Public tenders add their own entry requirements. They often ask for returnable schedules, evidence of insurances, WHS documentation, and sometimes prequalification or panel membership. Check the tender documents for exactly what is required before committing hours to the takeoff, because a missing returnable can rule a good price out.

How do you price a tender once you find it?

Pricing starts with a quantity takeoff: measuring every item from the drawings, then carrying those quantities into a bill of quantities priced against your own rates. The method is the same for a Sydney builder package and a regional council contract, and it is set out step by step in how to do a landscape quantity takeoff and how to price a commercial landscape tender.

Sydney jobs carry conditions that belong in the price rather than in a later argument. Rock under shallow topsoil, restricted access and out-of-hours work on constrained sites are usually found in the geotechnical information, the preliminaries and the conditions of contract, so read those before pricing and qualify anything the documents do not define.

Frequently asked questions

Where are NSW Government landscape tenders advertised?

NSW Government opportunities are published on buy.nsw. The old NSW eTendering site at tenders.nsw.gov.au now redirects there. Register as a supplier, then search and save alerts for landscaping, grounds maintenance, turf management, streetscape and open space work.

Do NSW councils use buy.nsw for their tenders?

Not as a rule. NSW councils advertise tenders on their own websites and through third-party portals such as VendorPanel, TenderLink and Tenders.net. Each council chooses its own channel, so check the tenders page of every council you want to work for.

Where do Sydney builders send out landscape packages?

Most private commercial landscape packages in Sydney are sent out through EstimateOne, which connects head contractors with subcontractors and has a landscaping tenders category. The rest come as direct invitations from builders who already know your work and your pricing.

Are tender aggregators worth paying for?

They can be if you chase public work across many councils. Aggregators such as Australian Tenders, illion TenderLink and Tenders.net republish public tenders by category and region with paid alerts. Weigh the subscription against the time spent checking each council yourself.

Can a Melbourne estimator price a Sydney landscape tender?

Yes. A landscape takeoff is measured from the drawings and specification, so the estimator does not need to be in Sydney. Local conditions such as rock, access and site constraints are read from the documents and qualified in the submission.

Key takeaways

  • Landscape tenders in NSW split into public work from government and councils, and private packages from builders.
  • NSW Government tenders are on buy.nsw, which replaced the old eTendering site.
  • Councils use their own websites and portals such as VendorPanel, TenderLink and Tenders.net, so check each one.
  • Most private landscape tenders in Sydney come through EstimateOne or direct builder invitations.
  • Set alerts by category and region on every channel, then qualify each tender before pricing it.
