Where do you find landscape tenders in Melbourne and Victoria?

Landscape tenders in Victoria and Melbourne come from five places: the Buying for Victoria Tenders Portal for state work including Parks Victoria, government registers and panels, council websites and portals such as MAV TenderLink, VendorPanel and eProcure, EstimateOne for private builder packages, and paid aggregators. What kinds of landscape work go to tender in Victoria?

Landscape tenders in Victoria fall into two worlds. Public tenders come from state government agencies and councils, and are often for landscape construction, streetscapes, parks and grounds maintenance contracts. Private commercial tenders come from head contractors who invite landscape subcontractors to price the landscape package on a building project.

The two worlds are found in different places and won in different ways. Public tenders are advertised openly on portals and judged against published criteria and returnable schedules. Private packages mostly travel through EstimateOne and through builders who already know you, so relationships matter as much as registrations.

This guide is the Victorian version of our national guide to finding landscape tenders. If you also work across the border, the guide to landscape tenders in NSW covers buy.nsw and NSW councils.

Where are government landscape tenders in Victoria advertised?

The Buying for Victoria Tenders Portal is where Victorian Government tenders are advertised. It sits at tenders.vic.gov.au and was formerly known as TendersVIC, so older references to TendersVIC point to the same place.

Suppliers register on the portal to view tender documents and respond. Once registered, you can search for landscaping, grounds maintenance, streetscape and open space work, and watch for new listings rather than stumbling across them after they close.

Parks Victoria advertises its open tenders through the same portal. That makes it a single place to watch for park-related landscape work alongside other state agencies, instead of checking agency websites one at a time.

What are registers and panels, and should you join them?

A register or panel is a pre-approved list of suppliers that a buyer draws on for a type of work, instead of running a fully open tender each time. Some Victorian Government work goes through registers and panels that suppliers apply to join, and the details are published at buyingfor.vic.gov.au.

Councils run panels too, for example civil works panels that cover hard and soft landscaping and streetscapes. Being on a panel can mean you are invited to quote on work that never appears as an open tender, so it is worth watching for panel applications as closely as for individual jobs.

Panel applications usually ask for more upfront evidence than a single tender, such as insurances, safety systems and capability. Check the application documents for what is required, and treat the effort as an investment in future invitations rather than a one-off bid.

Where do Victorian councils advertise landscape tenders?

Buying for Victoria does not cover council procurement, so council work has to be found separately. Victorian councils advertise tenders on their own websites and through e-tendering portals, including the TenderLink portal run in association with the Municipal Association of Victoria (MAV), VendorPanel and eProcure.

Councils do not all use the same portal, so the practical approach is to list the councils you want to work for and check each one's tenders page to see where it advertises. Then register on those portals, since registration is usually what lets you download documents, receive addenda and lodge a response.

Metropolitan Melbourne councils cover smaller, denser areas, so their landscape work often sits close to your yard and crews. Regional councils cover larger areas, where travel, cartage and local supply become bigger parts of the price. Both are worth watching, but be realistic about how far you can service a maintenance contract.

How do you find private landscape tenders in Melbourne?

EstimateOne (E1) is an Australian construction tendering platform that connects head contractors with subcontractors and suppliers. It has a landscaping tenders category, and it is where most private commercial landscape packages in Melbourne are sent out for pricing.

On E1, a builder tendering a project invites subcontractors to price each trade package, including landscaping. You see the project, the builders tendering it and the documents, and you submit your price to the builders you choose.

The other half of private work is direct invitations from builders you already work with. These never appear on a public list. A reliable price, a clear scope of what is included, and on-time submissions are what keep a builder sending you the next package.

Are tender aggregators and AusTender worth using?

A tender aggregator is a service that collects public tenders from many portals and republishes them by category and region, usually as paid alerts. The main ones are Australian Tenders, illion TenderLink and Tenders.net.

Aggregators save you checking dozens of council and agency pages yourself. They do not replace registering on the source portal, because that is where you normally download the full documents, receive addenda and lodge your response.

AusTender lists Australian Government (Commonwealth) tenders. Commonwealth landscape work in Victoria is advertised there rather than on the state portal, so add it to your list if you want that work.

How do you set up tender alerts that actually work?

Alerts turn tender hunting from a weekly chore into a steady feed. Set them up once, on every portal you register with, using the same categories and regions.

  1. Register on Buying for Victoria, EstimateOne and the council portals your target councils use.
  2. Save searches by category: landscaping, grounds maintenance, streetscape and open space.
  3. Save searches by region: metropolitan Melbourne, plus any regional areas you can genuinely service.
  4. Add panel notices so you see register and panel applications, not only single tenders.
  5. Review alerts on a set day each week and run each new tender through a quick bid or no-bid check.

Keep the categories broad at first. A tender described as open space renewal or streetscape upgrade may be mostly landscaping, and a narrow keyword search would miss it.

How do you qualify a Victorian tender before pricing it?

Qualifying a tender means deciding whether it is worth your pricing hours before you start the takeoff. Scope clarity, competition, fit, risk and deadline all matter, and our guide on whether a landscape tender is worth bidding walks through each check.

Public tenders add compliance on top. They often require returnable schedules, insurances and WHS documentation, and some require prequalification or panel membership. Check the tender documents for exactly what is required, because a missing schedule can rule out an otherwise good price.

Read the scope carefully at this stage too. Our guide on how to read a landscape tender scope covers what to look for, and gaps are far cheaper to query before you price than after you win.

How do you price a tender once you have found it?

Pricing starts with the quantities. A landscape quantity takeoff measures every item from the drawings, and those quantities become a bill of quantities that you price against your own rates, plus preliminaries for site costs that do not sit in any single line item.

For regional Victorian work, travel, accommodation, cartage and local supply need their own lines. Take those costs from your own job records and from local supplier quotes, not from a metropolitan job. We have notes on pricing work in Geelong, Ballarat and Bendigo.

Frequently asked questions

Where are Victorian Government landscape tenders advertised?

Victorian Government tenders are advertised on the Buying for Victoria Tenders Portal at tenders.vic.gov.au, which replaced TendersVIC. Parks Victoria advertises its open tenders there too. You register as a supplier to view the documents and respond, and you can save searches for landscaping work.

Do Victorian councils use Buying for Victoria?

No. Buying for Victoria does not cover council procurement. Victorian councils advertise tenders on their own websites and through e-tendering portals such as the TenderLink portal run with the Municipal Association of Victoria, VendorPanel and eProcure, so check each council you want to work for.

Where do Melbourne builders post landscape packages?

Most private commercial landscape packages in Melbourne come through EstimateOne, where head contractors invite subcontractors to price trade packages on their projects. The rest arrive as direct invitations from builders you already work with, which is why those relationships are worth looking after.

Are paid tender alert services worth it in Victoria?

They can be. Aggregators such as Australian Tenders, illion TenderLink and Tenders.net republish public tenders by category and region, which saves checking many portals. They do not replace registering on the portals themselves, because that is usually where you download documents and lodge your response.

Can a Melbourne estimator price regional Victorian tenders?

Yes. A quantity takeoff and priced bill of quantities are built from the drawings and specification, so the estimator does not need to be on site. What changes regionally is travel, cartage and supply, which you price from your own costs and local supplier quotes.

Key takeaways

  • Victorian landscape tenders come from two worlds: public tenders from government and councils, and private packages from head contractors.
  • State work, including Parks Victoria, is advertised on the Buying for Victoria Tenders Portal.
  • Council work is not on Buying for Victoria: check council websites, MAV TenderLink, VendorPanel and eProcure.
  • Most private Melbourne landscape packages come through EstimateOne or direct builder invitations.
  • Register on each portal, set alerts by category and region, and join panels where you can.
  • Qualify every tender before pricing, then price it from a proper takeoff against your own rates.
